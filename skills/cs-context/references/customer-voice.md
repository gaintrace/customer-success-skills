# Customer Voice — warmth, and the disclosure firewall

> Read this before writing **any** customer-facing text: a recap, an outreach email, a QBR
> slide, an escalation note, a renewal proposal, a reference request, an in-app message.
>
> It covers two things that fail together. The first is **warmth** — the register the best
> customer success people have always written in, which is not the same as friendliness and is
> the opposite of corporate. The second is the **disclosure firewall** — the mechanism that
> stops a health score, a risk band, an ARR-at-risk figure or a save play from reaching the
> customer. They belong in one file because the leak almost always happens *while* someone is
> trying to sound warm and reaches for a phrase carrying internal freight.

# Part 1 — Warmth

## What warmth actually is

Warmth is not exclamation marks, not "Hope you're having a great week!", and not enthusiasm.
Those are the *performance* of warmth, and customers read them as filler — a signal that the
sender had nothing specific to say and padded the gap.

Real warmth is the reader's sense that **a particular person paid attention to their
particular situation and is on their side.** It is produced by specificity, not by adjectives.

> "Hi Dana — hope all's well! Just wanted to check in and see how things are going with the
> platform. Let me know if there's anything we can help with!"

> "Hi Dana — your team got the Q3 close done in four days instead of the eleven it took in
> April, which is the thing you said you'd judge this on. The one piece still manual is the
> intercompany step; I've got a way to take that out and it needs about twenty minutes of
> your time. Worth doing before the Q4 close?"

The second is warmer, and it contains no warm words. It is warmer because it proves someone
was paying attention, it leads with the reader's win rather than the sender's need, and it
asks for something specific and small.

**The test:** could this sentence have been sent to any of forty customers? If yes, it is not
warm, however friendly it sounds. Specificity *is* the warmth.

## The six moves

| Move | What it does | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Lead with their outcome, not our activity** | Puts the reader at the centre of their own email | Not "I wanted to share our new reporting feature" but "You mentioned board prep eats your first week of every quarter — this takes most of that out" |
| **2. Name the person's actual stake** | Shows you know what *they* are measured on, not just what their company bought | "This is the number your CFO asks you about in November" |
| **3. Use their words** | Quoting the customer back to themselves is the strongest possible proof of listening | "You called it 'the Thursday scramble' — that's the bit this removes" |
| **4. Give before you ask** | Warmth is directional. Arrive with something | Send the analysis, the intro, the workaround, the benchmark — *then* make the ask |
| **5. Make the ask small and specific** | Respects their time and is easy to say yes to | "Twenty minutes on Thursday or Friday?" beats "Let me know if you'd like to discuss" |
| **6. Be brief on purpose, and say why when you are not** | Brevity is a form of respect. Length without reason reads as self-indulgence | If it must be long: "Longer than usual — three decisions in here and I didn't want to split them" |

## Sentence-level rewrites

| Instead of | Write | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I hope this email finds you well." | *(delete — start with the substance)* | It costs the reader a line and says nothing. The warmest thing you can do with the first line is use it. |
| "Just wanted to check in and see how things are going." | "Your team's usage of the approvals workflow jumped 40% in July — did something change on your side worth knowing about?" | Specific observation, genuine curiosity, easy to answer |
| "As per my last email…" | "Following up on the security questionnaire — happy to fill in the parts we can answer to save your team time." | Removes the reproach; offers help instead of pressure |
| "We're excited to announce…" | "This solves the export problem your team raised in March." | Their problem, not our announcement |
| "Please don't hesitate to reach out." | "If the migration hits anything unexpected, message me directly — I'll pick it up same day." | A real, bounded promise instead of a formality |
| "I'll circle back on that." | "I'll have an answer from our product team by Thursday, including if the answer is no." | A date, an owner, and honesty about the possible outcome |
| "Apologies for the delay." | "Sorry — that sat with me four days longer than it should have. Here it is, and here's what I've changed so it doesn't happen again." | Owns it specifically rather than gesturing at it |
| "Per our conversation, please find attached…" | "Here's the pricing we talked through, with the two-year option you asked me to price up." | Human, and it proves you listened |
| "We value your partnership." | "You've been with us three years and pushed us on things we needed pushing on — the audit-log work came from you." | Evidence of the relationship instead of an assertion of it |
| "Let me know your thoughts." | "Does that timeline work, or should I build it around your close week instead?" | A closed question is easier to answer than an open invitation |
| "Unfortunately, we're unable to accommodate that request." | "We're not going to build that — here's why, and here's the closest thing we can do." | Plain, respectful, and it leaves them something |
| "Can you loop in the relevant stakeholder?" | "Is Marcus the right person for the security review, or has that moved?" | Names a human; assumes nothing |

## Openers and closers

**Openers.** The first line decides whether the rest is read. Three that work:

1. **The observation** — "Your Finance team came onto the platform last week, which is the thing we'd been waiting on since January."
2. **The delivery** — "Here's the migration plan, and the one decision I need from you is in the second paragraph."
3. **The acknowledgment** — "You were right about the export limits, and we got it wrong. Here's what's changing."

Never open with "I". Never open with the weather, the season, or the quarter. Never open with
an apology for emailing.

**Closers.** One ask, dated, easy.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "Let me know if you'd like to set something up." | "Thursday 2pm or Friday 11am — either works my end." |
| "Looking forward to hearing from you." | "If I don't hear back by Friday I'll assume the timeline works and get it scheduled." |
| "Thanks in advance!" | "Thanks — I know the close week is brutal." |

**Sign-offs.** "Thanks," for an established relationship. "Best," for newer or more senior
audiences. Match the customer's own register if they have set one. Never "Warm regards",
never "Cheers" to someone you have not met, never a quote in the signature.

## Register by relationship

| Relationship | Register | The specific trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Established** (>6 months, regular contact) | Conversational, first names, one line of personality | Over-familiarity in a thread they will forward to their CFO |
| **New** (first 90 days, or a new stakeholder) | Friendly but measured, no shorthand, acronyms spelled out | Assuming context they do not have — they were not part of the original decision |
| **Executive** (VP+, or a forwarded audience) | BLUF, no preamble, outcomes and money, half the length | Burying the ask under the recap |
| **Difficult** (friction surfaced, escalation live) | Direct, plain, zero excess apology, no hedging verbs | Cheerfulness — it reads as not having heard them |
| **Multi-party** (mixed seniority on the thread) | Write to the most senior reader; detail in tables the rest can scan | Addressing the friendliest person rather than the decision-maker |
| **Technical** (engineers, admins, security) | Precise, IDs and version numbers, no adjectives | Marketing language — this audience discounts it instantly and permanently |

## Warmth under pressure

The hardest and most valuable case. When something has gone wrong, warmth is **not** softness —
it is the willingness to be plain.

| Situation | The warm move | The mistake that looks warm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We broke something | Name it in the first sentence, quantify their cost, say what changes | Leading with context and explanation, which reads as defence |
| We are saying no | Say no in the first line, then the reasoning, then the nearest alternative | "It's on the roadmap" when it is not — the kindest-sounding sentence in CS and the most damaging |
| They are angry | Reflect their words back accurately before responding at all | Rushing to solutions, which tells them the complaint was an obstacle |
| We missed a commitment | State the date you missed and the new date, with an owner | An apology with no new date |
| They are churning | Thank them plainly, make leaving easy, keep the door open | Pressure, guilt, or a surprise retention offer at the exit |
| Price is going up | Give maximum notice, explain the change, name what they get | Burying it in a contract amendment |

**Never apologise more than once for the same thing.** A second apology asks the customer to
absolve you, which makes your feelings their job.

## The banned phrasebook

These are the tells of an artifact nobody thought about. They are banned in every
customer-facing output in this library.

| Banned | Why | Use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Just checking in" / "Touching base" / "Circling back" | No content; a request for attention with nothing offered | Lead with a specific observation |
| "Hope you're well" / "Hope this finds you well" | A wasted first line | The substance |
| "As per my last email" | Reproach | "Following up on X — anything I can do to unblock it?" |
| "Reaching out" | Filler | Just say the thing |
| "Synergies", "leverage", "utilise", "bandwidth", "circle up", "socialise" | Corporate register; distances you from a person | "use", "time", "share", "talk through" |
| "Drive adoption" / "ensure success" / "align" | Vendor-speak about the customer as an object | Say what you will do and what changes for them |
| "We value your partnership" | An assertion where evidence belongs | A specific thing the relationship produced |
| "Exciting news!" / "We're thrilled" | Our feelings, not their outcome | What it does for them |
| "Quick question" (when it is not) | Mildly dishonest; sets a false expectation | Say how long it will actually take |
| "Let me know if you have any questions" | Ritual | The one thing you actually want them to do |
| "At your earliest convenience" | Vague pressure | A date |
| "Per our SLA" | Correct and cold — it invokes a contract mid-relationship | State what will happen and when |
| Exclamation marks in a difficult thread | Reads as not having understood the situation | Plain full stops |

# Part 2 — The disclosure firewall

Every account carries a body of internal assessment — a health score, a risk band, ARR at
risk, a save play, a forecast category, a note about which of their executives is a blocker.
None of it may reach the customer. A leaked risk score has ended renewals that were not
otherwise at risk, because the customer learns two things at once: that you had been grading
them, and that you did not think enough of them to say so.

## Three classes

Classify **every line** before it goes anywhere near a customer-facing draft.

| Class | Definition | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **INTERNAL-ONLY** | Our assessment, our commercial position, our opinion of their people, anything expressed in risk or revenue terms | The internal note. Never translated, never hinted at |
| **TRANSLATE** | A real observation that the customer should hear, but currently phrased in internal language | Rewrite into the customer's frame, then it may be sent |
| **SHARE** | Facts the customer already knows or said, decisions made on the call, commitments, dates | Goes as-is |

Default to INTERNAL-ONLY. A line moves to SHARE only when you can say who on the customer's
side already knows it, or when it is a commitment we are making.

## The never-list

These never appear in customer-facing text, in any wording, however softened:

- Health score, risk score, risk band, "at risk", "red account", "green", churn, churn risk
- ARR at risk, exposure, dollars at risk, lifetime value, cost to serve, margin
- Forecast category, commit/best-case/omitted, close date, probability, renewal likelihood
- Save play, rescue play, war room, escalation tier, "we escalated this internally"
- Any assessment of a named person: blocker, detractor, weak champion, "not technical enough"
- Champion-departure inferences ("we noticed Jamie left")
- Internal capacity or coverage language: book size, tech-touch, pooled, "your tier"
- Competitor intelligence they did not tell us
- Commitments not yet internally agreed, and roadmap dates we do not own
- Anything from another customer, named or identifiable
- Anything a colleague said in confidence, and anything from a call recording they did not know was analysed
- Our own internal blame ("support dropped this")

## The translation table

Most INTERNAL-ONLY lines have a legitimate TRANSLATE form. The observation is often real and
worth raising — the framing is what has to change.

| Internal | Customer-facing |
| --- | --- |
| "Health score fell from 78 to 41" | *(never — the score itself is not translatable)* |
| "Usage is down 62%, account at risk" | "The reporting module's gone quiet since July after running steady since March — I'd rather ask than assume. Did something change your end?" |
| "Champion departed; we're single-threaded" | "I want to make sure we're supporting the right people now — who's picking up the work Jamie was leading?" |
| "Licence utilisation 43%, downsell risk" | "You're using 86 of 200 seats. Worth looking at whether that's a rollout gap we can help close, or whether the number should just be smaller at renewal." |
| "No exec sponsor; multithreading depth 1" | "It'd help to have twenty minutes with whoever owns this outcome at your end — partly so you're not the only one carrying it internally." |
| "ARR at risk $148k" | *(never)* |
| "Save play activated; exec engaged" | "I've pulled in our VP of Customer Success — she's got authority I don't on the timeline." |
| "Forecast moved to Best Case" | *(never)* |
| "They're evaluating a competitor" | "If you're looking at other options, I'd rather be part of that conversation than outside it." |
| "Detractor NPS from their admin" | "Your feedback in the July survey was direct and fair. Here's what changed because of it." |
| "Onboarding overran; first-year churn risk" | "We're three weeks past where we said we'd be. Here's the revised plan and what I'm doing differently." |
| "Low-touch tier, no QBR entitlement" | "Here's the quarterly summary — and if you ever want to walk through it live, just ask." |
| "Escalated to engineering, P1 aged 19 days" | "This is with a named engineer now — Sam owns it, and you'll get an update from me every Tuesday until it's closed." |

Two rules for translation: **never translate a number the customer cannot verify**, and
**never translate an inference into a statement of fact**. "We noticed Jamie left" asserts
something you inferred from a bounce; "who's picking up Jamie's work?" asks.

## The leak scan

Run this over every customer-facing draft, mechanically, before it goes out. Any hit is
**rewritten, not softened** — softening internal language leaves the shape of it visible.

1. **Vocabulary scan** — search the draft for: `risk` · `at-risk` · `churn` · `health` ·
   `score` · `red` / `green` / `amber` · `escalat` · `save` · `forecast` · `commit` ·
   `probability` · `ARR` · `exposure` · `retention` · `renewal likelihood` · `play` ·
   `tier` · `book` · `blocker` · `detractor` · `sentiment`.
2. **Number scan** — every figure must be one the customer can verify in their own systems or
   one they gave us. Delete any internal metric.
3. **Person scan** — every named person is either on the thread or was named by the customer.
   No assessment of anyone.
4. **Inference scan** — anything not directly observed is phrased as a question, not a claim.
5. **Commitment scan** — every commitment has an internally-agreed owner. No roadmap dates we
   do not own.
6. **Third-party scan** — no competitor, no other customer, no partner the customer did not raise.
7. **Forward test** — assume this is forwarded to their CFO, their procurement lead, and a
   competitor's rep. Does anything embarrass us or them?
8. **Read-aloud test** — read the first three lines aloud. If they sound like a template or a
   vendor, rewrite them.

## The separator rule — and the copy block

Customer-facing text is **always emitted inside a fenced code block**, so the reader can copy
it in one gesture with its formatting intact — line breaks, indentation, table alignment, blank
lines between paragraphs. Prose emitted as normal markdown gets reflowed by the terminal, loses
its line breaks on copy, and arrives in the customer's inbox as a wall.

Two rules, together:

1. **Fence it.** Use a ```` ```text ```` fence for anything destined for an email client, a
   Slack message, or a form field. Everything inside the fence is send-ready as written —
   no placeholders left unfilled, no editorial notes, no internal language.
2. **Separate it.** Precede the fence with an unmissable divider, so a reader skimming to the
   bottom cannot mistake internal analysis for the draft.

````
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Northwind Q3 sync — SSO rollout decision + 3 owners

Hi Dana,

Your team closed Q3 in four days against eleven in April — that's the
number you said you'd judge this on, and it moved.

Decided today:
  • SSO-first rollout, not API-first. Marcus owns the IdP config.
  • Finance onboarding moves to w/c 22 Sept, after your close week.

Who owes what:
  • Me — revised rollout plan, Thu 4 Sept
  • Marcus — Okta app registration, Fri 5 Sept
  • You — confirm the Finance contact, Wed 3 Sept

Still open: whether the audit-log export needs to be in scope for Q4.
You were going to check with Ravi — no rush before Thursday.

Next session: Thu 18 Sept 2pm, or Fri 19th 11am. Either works my end.

Thanks — I know close week is brutal.

Jo
```
````

**Formatting inside the block.** Write for an email client, not for a markdown renderer:

| Do | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Plain text, blank line between paragraphs | Markdown headings (`##`) — they arrive as literal hashes |
| `•` or `-` bullets, indented two spaces | Nested markdown lists |
| A blank line before and after each list | Bold via `**` — it arrives as asterisks in plain-text clients |
| Aligned columns with spaces if a table is unavoidable | Pipe tables — they arrive as pipes |
| Real dates, real names, real numbers | `[Name]`, `[Date]`, `[Account]` placeholders |
| One fence per artifact | Several messages crammed into one fence |

If more than one artifact is being produced — an email plus a Slack message, or a message to
two different stakeholders — give each its own fence with its own one-line label above it.

**Placeholders are a failure, not a courtesy.** If a name or a date is genuinely unavailable,
do not emit `[Name]` inside the send-ready block. Emit the block without that sentence and
raise the gap above the divider as `UNKNOWN — requires X`. A block containing a placeholder is
not send-ready, and the most common way an unedited template reaches a customer is that it
looked finished.

## Failure cases worth knowing

| How the leak happens | The guard |
| --- | --- |
| Copy-pasting a bullet from the internal note into the recap because it was well phrased | Rewrite every line for the customer draft; never move a line across the wall |
| Forwarding a thread that has internal replies below the fold | Start a new email to the customer. Never forward an internal thread |
| Screen-sharing a CRM or CS platform view on a call | Close every internal tab before the call, not during |
| A QBR slide built from the internal deck | Build customer decks from source data, not from the internal pack |
| Attaching an export that includes a health-score column | Check the columns of every attachment |
| An auto-generated calendar invite title: "Acme — save play" | Neutral titles on anything the customer can see |
| A shared Slack Connect channel used for an internal aside | Assume everything in a shared channel is read by the customer, because it is |
| Autocomplete putting the customer on an internal thread | Check the recipient line last, deliberately, every time |

## The pre-send checklist

- [ ] The first line is specific to this account and contains no filler
- [ ] Nothing in the banned phrasebook appears
- [ ] There is a specific observation only this customer's data could produce
- [ ] Their own words are used where they said something useful
- [ ] Length matches the substance; no padding
- [ ] One ask, dated, easy to say yes to
- [ ] Register matches the relationship and the most senior reader
- [ ] Vocabulary scan run — no risk, health, score, forecast, ARR, save, tier language
- [ ] Every number is verifiable by the customer
- [ ] Every inference is phrased as a question, not a claim
- [ ] Every commitment has an internally-agreed owner and a real date
- [ ] Forward test passed
- [ ] Customer-facing text sits inside a ```text fence, below the divider, copyable as-is
- [ ] No unfilled placeholders anywhere inside the fence
- [ ] Formatted for an email client, not a markdown renderer
