# The Commercial Position

## 1. The justification order

Choose the uplift internally at T-120, communicate it with the proposal at T-90, and never
introduce it for the first time inside T-45. Justify in this order, every time:

1. **Outcomes delivered, in their metrics** — the value pack from Step 6, in the numbers the
   customer validated, not the numbers the product reports.
2. **Product value shipped since the last renewal** — what they now have that they did not buy,
   restricted to what they have actually adopted. Shipped-but-unadopted is not an argument.
3. **Usage growth and entitlement true-up** — seats, volume, environments or entities beyond
   what the order form covers. This is a correction, not an increase, and it is priced as one.
4. **The contractual clause** — the CPI or fixed escalator that already exists.
5. **List-price movement** — what everyone now pays.

The order is the whole point. Leading with the clause turns a value conversation into a
procurement fight: the customer's only available move against a clause is to argue about the
clause, and the person who argues about clauses is procurement, not your champion. Leading with
outcomes gives the champion something to carry into their own budget review — the uplift becomes
the last item in a conversation about what the thing is worth, rather than the first item in a
conversation about what it costs.

Rungs 1 and 2 are the customer's reasons. Rungs 3, 4 and 5 are ours. Never open on ours.

## 2. The uplift ladder

Pick the highest rung you can produce the evidence for, then price it. A rung whose evidence you
cannot produce is not available to you this cycle, regardless of what finance has modelled.

| Rung | Position | Evidence you must be able to produce | What disqualifies it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hold at 0% | Account is At Risk, or the hold is traded for term length or reference rights | The trade, in writing | A hold given away with no get — that is a discount with extra steps, and it resets the baseline for every future cycle |
| Contractual index | Exercising an existing CPI or fixed escalator clause | The clause reference | No clause in the executed contract, or a clause whose index period has not been computed |
| Standard uplift | Clause plus a documented value-realisation summary | Both artifacts; never assert an uplift without the value artifact | No value pack delivered at T-150/T-120, or a pack with no customer-side validator |
| Above-standard | Materially expanded scope, shipped product value now adopted, or a documented below-market entry price | Usage delta plus the price benchmark | Adoption asserted rather than measured; "below market" with no benchmark on file |
| Restructure | Repackaging or true-up of under-licensed usage | Position as a restructure, not an increase | Entitlement data you cannot show them; a true-up presented as a price rise, which reads as a penalty and triggers an audit conversation |

**Two rules bind the whole ladder.** The first: never assert an uplift without the value
artifact — an increase with no evidence behind it is a test of the relationship, and the
customer will price it as one. The second: the rung is chosen before the number. Teams that
pick a percentage first and then hunt for a justification produce exactly the
justification-shopping the T-120/T-90 sequence exists to prevent.

**Where the account is At Risk** (`churn-risk` band, or an override floor fired), the ladder
collapses to Hold — and the hold is still traded, for term length, a reference, a case study,
a multi-year commitment or an executive sponsor meeting. An At Risk account given a free hold
learns that risk is a negotiating position.

## 3. What the 3–5% figure is and is not

Standard negotiated annual uplifts of 3–5%, often pegged to CPI or a fixed percentage
"whichever is higher", are reported by SaaS contracting guides (2026) — **practitioner
guidance, not a measured benchmark.** Use your own realised uplift history where you have it;
say so where you do not.

To build your own: take the last twenty closed renewals, compute realised uplift as
`(new ARR − co-termed prior ARR) / co-termed prior ARR` excluding new-scope expansion, and
segment it by ATR band and by whether a value pack was delivered before the price. The second
cut is the one worth having — it is the only internal evidence that the T-120/T-90 sequence
pays for itself, and it is the argument that wins the sequence its calendar time.

Until you have that, write the position as `standard uplift per policy` and record the source
of the policy. A borrowed benchmark stated as your own number is the same failure as a borrowed
lead time: precision that fits nobody.

## 4. The commercial-touch prerequisite (C25 · R11)

Under 365 days since the last commercial touch with the economic buyer or budget holder,
proceed. Over 365, none on record, or `UNKNOWN`: the plan carries a dated **commercial-context
conversation before T-120**, with an owner, and the T-120 price decision does not emit until
that row exists in the stage plan.

A *commercial touch* is a dated, logged conversation with the person who owns the budget line,
in which price, term, scope, budget or the contract itself was discussed. It is not a QBR that
never mentioned money, an adoption review, a support escalation call, or an email they did not
answer. The test is whether a third party reading the record could say what commercial subject
was raised and what the buyer said about it.

**R11 binds its timing.** The conversation is never attached to an apology, an outage, a credit
or a missed milestone. Where one of those landed inside 14 days either side, move the touch,
print the deferral and the new date, and never merge the two conversations. A price conversation
delivered on the back of an apology is heard as leverage being taken, and it is remembered that
way for the rest of the relationship.

**Why this is a hard prerequisite and not a nice-to-have.** A renewal ask that is the year's
first commercial conversation has been converted into a negotiation before anyone opens their
mouth: the buyer's first data point about the commercial relationship is a request for more
money. The fix is cheap — one thirty-minute conversation, six months early, whose only agenda
is what the next term should look like — and it is the highest-return half hour in the ladder.

## 5. Ownership — who owns price, who owns value

**The Renewal Manager owns price. The CSM owns value.** A CSM who negotiates price cannot be
the customer's advocate next time — the role depends on the customer believing the CSM's
account of what is working is not a sales position, and one price negotiation is enough to
end that belief permanently.

| | CSM owns | Renewal Manager owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value evidence pack (Step 6) | Yes | Contributes the commercial framing |
| The number, the rung, the walk-away | No | Yes |
| The T-150/T-120 EBR | Yes | Attends, does not price |
| The T-90 proposal and everything after | Contributes evidence | Yes |
| Concessions and their gets | No | Yes |
| Paper critical path (Step 8) | Escalates blockers | Yes |

**Where one person holds both roles** — the common case below enterprise — run two separate
conversations on different days, and say which one you are in: *"Today is the outcomes review;
we'll talk about next term's paperwork next week."* Naming the switch costs one sentence and
preserves the distinction the two-role split exists to protect.

## 6. The concession ladder — every give has a get

Decide the ladder before T-60, not inside the negotiation. Each row is a give that is only
available in exchange for its get, and the get is written into the order form, not agreed
verbally.

| Give | Get it is available for |
| --- | --- |
| Hold at 0% | Multi-year term, or a named reference / case study with their marketing's approval |
| Reduced uplift | Longer term, earlier signature date, or annual-upfront payment terms |
| Payment terms (net-60 → net-30 reversed) | Price held, or the PO raised before the order form issues |
| Additional seats or volume at the current unit price | Term extension, or a committed expansion date |
| A pilot of an additional module | A named business owner and a decision date, not an open evaluation |
| Bridge extension at current terms | Written confirmation of intent, and a signature date |

**Walk-away is a number, decided internally, written down before the first conversation.** A
walk-away invented mid-negotiation is not a walk-away, it is a floor discovered by the customer.

## 7. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Opening with the escalator clause | Open with outcomes in their metrics; the clause is rung 4, not rung 1 |
| Picking the percentage, then finding the justification | Pick the rung the evidence supports, then price it |
| Delivering the value pack and the uplift in one conversation | Value at T-150/T-120, price at T-90 (R11) |
| An uplift asserted with no value artifact | The Standard rung requires both artifacts; without the pack you are on the Contractual-index rung at best |
| A true-up presented as a price increase | Position it as a restructure, and show the entitlement data |
| Conceding without a get | Every give in §6 has a named get, written into the order form |
| A price conversation on the back of an outage or credit | R11 — move it, print the deferral and the new date |
| The CSM negotiating price | The Renewal Manager owns price; where one person holds both, two conversations on different days |
| Introducing a new expansion ask inside T-30 | It reads as opportunism. Post-renewal T+0 to T+60 is the legitimate window (`expansion-finder`) |
