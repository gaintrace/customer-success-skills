# Objective Discovery

> How to reach a business objective the customer's executive is actually measured on, rather than
> a product wish dressed up as a goal. Read this before the first planning conversation, before a
> re-baseline, and any time the objectives you hold name your own product.
>
> Evidence labels: `[M]` measured study · `[PROD]` published production configuration of a named
> company · `[V]` vendor research · `[P]` practitioner rule of thumb · `[A]` academic.

## 1. The failure this file prevents

Kristen Hayer names three failure modes and they are all discovery failures rather than writing
failures `[P]`:

| Failure | What it looks like | The discovery fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wrong builders** | A CSM builds the plan alone and presents it; from the customer's side it is someone else's document | Arrive with questions, not a draft. The conversation is the work |
| **Wrong focus** | Objectives are product-adoption milestones, feature usage and onboarding stages — things that matter to the vendor | Start from revenue targets, headcount, cost pressure and board expectations, then connect the product |
| **Wrong scope** | The plan restricts itself to metrics the vendor controls, because those are safe | The customer is already judging you against their real goals. Measuring only the safe ones signals self-protection |

Lincoln Murphy supplies the second half of the frame: a **Desired Outcome** is the customer's goal
plus the appropriate experience of reaching it, and a **Success Milestone** is "an action or event
marking a significant change or stage in the customer's journey toward their ever-evolving Desired
Outcome" `[P]`. His constraint on goals is the one that most often gets skipped: *Objective +
Conditions + Time Frame* make a goal, and **without a timeframe a goal is just a wish** `[P]`.

The **Success Gap** is the space between a customer finishing the tasks that make them look
successful in your product and actually achieving their outcome `[P]`. Everything below is aimed at
that gap.

## 2. Read three sources before you ask anything

Informed questions get better answers, and being visibly prepared is itself a warmth move. Budget
45–60 minutes.

| Source | Where it lives | What to extract | The line to use in the room |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Public commitments** | Annual report, shareholder letter, earnings call transcript, investor day deck, press releases, analyst-day slides, regulatory filings | The 3–5 priorities the company has told the market it is pursuing this year, with the numbers attached | "Your Q2 letter names cost-to-serve per claim, down 15%. Is that yours, or does it sit with someone else?" |
| **Their internal artifacts** | The operating plan, the page of the board deck their function owns, the original business case that funded the purchase, an OKR sheet, a job posting | The version of the corporate priority that has been handed to *this* executive, and the number on it | "The business case we were bought on assumed 11 days to close. Is that still the number you are working against?" |
| **Our own record** | The sales-cycle notes, the MEDDPICC fields, the discovery deck, the security questionnaire, the first 90 days of tickets | What they said they were buying, who said it, and whether that person is still there | "When you bought this, Ravi framed it as a close-cycle problem. Is that still how you would describe it?" |

**Job postings are underused.** A company hiring six claims-automation engineers has told you where
the budget went. A company that has stopped hiring in a function has told you something else.

**Rule:** quote what you found, with the date, and invite correction. Being corrected produces a
better objective than being right, and it converts the plan from yours into theirs in one move.

## 3. The "so that" ladder, with branch cases

Climb from whatever they say until you reach a number on somebody's scorecard. Stop **one rung
below the company mission** — a mission has no baseline and cannot be measured.

| Rung | Example | Your next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 · Feature request | "We need the new dashboard" | "So that what changes for you?" |
| 1 · Capability | "Regional managers can see claims volume daily" | "So they can do what they cannot do today?" |
| 2 · Behaviour change | "They rebalance queues before the backlog builds" | "And when that works, what number moves?" |
| 3 · Operational metric | "Backlog age drops, so cost per claim drops" | "What is cost per claim today, and what does it need to be?" |
| **4 · Business objective** | "Cost to serve per claim, 15% over plan, and I own it" | **Stop.** Baseline, target, date, owner |
| 5 · Mission | "Be the most efficient insurer in the market" | Too high. Go back to rung 4 |

**Branch cases — what to do when the ladder does not climb cleanly:**

| Situation | What it means | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They cannot answer "so that what?" | The request came from a user, not from a plan | Ask who asked for it and what problem they described. Climb from there, or park it as an initiative under an existing objective |
| Every answer is about a person, not a number | The objective is political — someone needs to be seen to deliver | Legitimate, and worth naming privately. Find the number that person will be judged on, because that is the real objective |
| The answer jumps straight to the mission | They are pitching you rather than planning | Ask "which part of that lands on your team this year?" |
| The number is a cost their finance team owns, not them | You have the wrong stakeholder for this objective | Ask for a 20-minute introduction to the owner, with the champion in the room |
| The number moves for reasons they cannot influence | It is an outcome, not an objective they can be held to | Climb *down* one rung to the operational metric they control |
| They name a compliance deadline | This is a date-driven objective and the strongest kind | Treat the regulatory date as the target date; it will not move |

## 4. The question bank

Three per conversation, maximum. Ask one, then stop talking — the second half of the answer arrives
after the silence.

### 4.1 Opening the objective

| Question | What it surfaces | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What has to be true by <their fiscal year end> for this year to have gone well for your team?" | The objective in their frame, not ours | Time-bound, team-scoped, no product in it |
| "Which number on your team's scorecard is furthest from where it needs to be?" | Priority ordering, and the pain | Executives track a small number of metrics; this asks for the worst one |
| "When your CFO reviews this line item in <month>, what will they want to see?" | The renewal criterion, a year early | Puts the buyer in the room without mentioning renewal |
| "What did you promise when you got this budget approved?" | The original business case, often forgotten internally | The most direct route to the objective, and it flatters |
| "If we do nothing else this year but one thing, what should it be?" | Forces ranking | A plan with a top objective beats a plan with five equal ones |

### 4.2 Making it measurable

| Question | What it surfaces |
| --- | --- |
| "What is that number today, and how do you measure it?" | Baseline and measurement source in one answer |
| "Who pulls that number, and how often?" | The measurement owner — the person who has to re-run it every month |
| "What would you consider a good result, and what would be a disappointment?" | A target range; take the lower end as the target |
| "Has anyone tried to move this before? What happened?" | Prior failures, dependencies, political history |
| "What else is moving that number at the same time as us?" | Confounders, and the attribution conversation before it is contested |

### 4.3 Ownership and feasibility

| Question | What it surfaces |
| --- | --- |
| "Whose objectives is this written into for this period?" | Whether a named person is graded on it. If nobody, the objective is decorative |
| "Who on your side would be doing the work, and what else are they doing this quarter?" | SME capacity, the most common silent dependency |
| "What has to happen on your side that has nothing to do with us?" | Their internal dependencies — change boards, freezes, approvals |
| "Are there windows in the year when this cannot move?" | Freeze windows, peak season, audit and close periods |
| "Who could stop this, even if everyone here agrees?" | The unmet approver — security, legal, works council, clinical |

### 4.4 The executive-level questions

| Question | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| "What are you being asked to do differently this year compared with last?" | Meeting a new sponsor |
| "Where does this programme sit against the other things competing for the same budget?" | Sizing how defensible the line item is |
| "If this works, what does it let you do next?" | Surfacing the expansion path without asking for one |
| "What would make you cancel this?" | Direct, and executives respect it. Names the risk register for you |

## 5. Reaching the metric they are measured on

An objective a named executive is graded on survives a budget review. An objective nobody is graded
on does not, however sensible it is. Three routes to that metric, in order of reliability:

| Route | How | Reliability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **They tell you** | Ask directly: "what are you measured on this year?" Most executives answer straightforwardly | High |
| **The document says** | The operating plan, the OKR sheet, the board page, or the public commitment their function owns | High, but confirm the version is current |
| **You infer it** | From the pattern of what they escalate, what they ask for first, and what they cancel meetings for | Medium. Label the inference, state the rule, ask them to correct it |

**The compensation test, applied.** For each candidate objective, answer in one line: *whose
performance review contains this, and what happens to them if it is missed?* If the answer is "no
one's" or "unclear", the objective is not ready. Either find the owner, or reduce the objective to
one the champion is graded on and note that the sponsor connection is missing — that gap is a risk
register row, not a reason to invent an owner.

**The money test.** Every objective must be convertible to currency with a unit economic the
customer supplies: cost per ticket, cost per claim, fully loaded hourly rate, average deal value,
revenue per store. Ask for it during discovery, not at the renewal — asking a year later reads as
building a case rather than running a plan. Take the low end of any range they give.

**The 30-day test.** For each objective, name a leading indicator that will move within 30 days.
If none exists, you will learn the objective failed a quarter after it did.

## 6. The wrong-answer catalogue

Product wishes that arrive dressed as objectives. Each has a legitimate objective underneath it.

| What they say | Why it is not an objective | Climb to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Get everyone on the platform" | Adoption is our metric, not theirs | The work the platform replaces, and what that work costs today |
| "Use the API more" | Product-centric vanity | The manual transfers being automated, in hours and error rate |
| "Roll out to more teams" | No count, no date, no definition of rolled out | Named teams, headcount, a live definition, and the outcome each team owns |
| "Improve reporting" | Not specific, not measurable, no relevance chain | The decision that is currently made late or badly because the report arrives slowly |
| "Better visibility" | A feeling | The decision the visibility would change, and who makes it |
| "Get executive buy-in" | An activity, not an outcome | A named sponsor and a signed charter, with a date |
| "Increase adoption" | Ours, and unmeasurable as stated | The population, the counted event, the baseline and the target |
| "Consolidate tools" | Could be theirs — check | The contracts being retired and their annual cost, with the retirement dates |
| "Train the team" | An input | The task the trained team performs, and the throughput or quality change |
| "Be more data-driven" | A slogan | The specific decision that will be made from data, by whom, on what cadence |

## 7. Three stakeholders, three answers

Ask the economic buyer, the champion and a power user separately. They will not agree, and the
disagreement is information.

| Stakeholder | The objective they name | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Economic buyer** | Cost, revenue, risk, or a board commitment | Abstract; may not know the operational reality. Ask what number they would quote to their own CFO |
| **Champion** | The programme they own, in operational terms | Sometimes the objective is their promotion case. Legitimate; find the underlying business number |
| **Power user** | The friction they live with daily | Real and useful, but rarely a business objective on its own. It becomes a leading indicator or an initiative |

**Reconciliation rule.** Where they conflict, the plan is graded on the **economic buyer's**
objective; the others become supporting outcomes beneath it. Never average three answers into a
sentence nobody said — a plan the buyer does not recognise fails at exactly the wrong moment.
Record all three in the plan, attributed, with dates.

## 8. When the customer has no articulated objective

This is common and it is not a dead end. Three cases:

| Case | What you are seeing | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **They have one but cannot phrase it** | Answers are anecdotes and complaints | Reflect back: "It sounds like the thing that would matter most is <X>. Is that fair?" Offer a draft to be corrected, not accepted |
| **They genuinely have none** | The purchase was opportunistic, or the sponsor who had one has left | The first objective becomes *establish the objective and the baseline*, with a date, an owner and a working session |
| **They have one they will not share** | Political, confidential, or pre-announcement | Ask what you can be told, plan against the operational metric, and note the gap. Do not guess it |

In all three, the plan still gets written. What changes is the state: objectives stay `Proposed`
and the plan carries a confidence cap until the customer has agreed the objective, the baseline,
the success criteria and the timeline.

## 9. Four full outcome chains

One per buying persona. Each chain must be unbroken: if you cannot draw a line from the product
behaviour to the business objective, the goal is not ready. Every chain below states its unit
economic, its customer-set attribution and the dependency that breaks it — and none of them may be
written as a goal until the baseline gate in `../SKILL.md` Step 3 is cleared.

### 9.1 VP Claims Operations — cost to serve

| Level | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Business objective | Cut cost to serve per claim by 15% in FY27 — named in their Q2 shareholder letter, owned by D. Okafor |
| Measurable outcome | Tier-1 support tickets per 1,000 active accounts per month: 412 → 290 |
| Leading indicator | Help-widget impressions on newly covered surfaces ≥4,000/week by week 6; deflection ≥25% within 3 weeks of each surface launch |
| Product behaviour | Widget deployed on 11 of 11 surfaces; knowledge articles published for 4 of 4 top ticket topics |
| Unit economic | $18.40 fully loaded per Tier-1 ticket, customer-supplied, low end of their range |
| Attribution | 70%, set by the customer; remainder to their own knowledge-base rewrite |
| Dependency | Their front-end release train runs 15 Sep and 15 Nov only |

### 9.2 CFO / Group Controller — close cycle

| Level | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Business objective | Close the books in 4 business days, from 11 at the April close — the metric their audit committee sees |
| Measurable outcome | Median days-to-close per period: 11 → 4 |
| Leading indicator | Share of intercompany journals auto-matched ≥85% by week 3 of the first cycle |
| Product behaviour | Reconciliation rules configured for all 6 entities; 3 of 3 ERP connectors live and syncing daily |
| Unit economic | 7 days × 9 finance staff × loaded daily rate, customer-supplied |
| Attribution | 60%; a parallel process redesign owns the rest, and they set the split |
| Dependency | Entity 6 migrates ERP in Q3 — exclude it from the measurement window and say so on the artifact |

### 9.3 CRO / VP Sales — win rate

| Level | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Business objective | Raise win rate by 300bps in FY27 — from the sales kickoff deck they shared on 12 Aug |
| Measurable outcome | Win rate on qualified opportunities (stage ≥3, ≥$25k): +5pts in matched regions |
| Leading indicator | Proposal-module usage ≥50% of qualified opportunities within 6 weeks of each regional launch |
| Product behaviour | All 7 regions enabled; ≥1 proposal generated per rep within 14 days of enablement |
| Unit economic | Average deal value $86k, customer-supplied |
| Attribution | 40%, customer-set; a pricing change and a methodology rollout take the remainder |
| Dependency | Confounded by a 1 Jan pricing change — hold two regions as a staged control through Q1 and say so |

### 9.4 VP Engineering / CISO — release reliability

| Level | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Business objective | Remove release delays as a board-reported risk by the Q3 review — their internal risk register entry, shared 4 Sep |
| Measurable outcome | Failure rate on the main branch: ~30% → under 5%, with the root cause documented and shared with their engineering lead |
| Leading indicator | Failed-pipeline reruns per week trending down for 3 consecutive weeks; median time to green under 25 minutes |
| Product behaviour | Required checks enabled on all protected branches; flaky-test quarantine configured; 12 of 12 new-team developers with ≥1 pipeline running |
| Unit economic | Engineer hours lost per failed release × loaded hourly rate, customer-supplied |
| Attribution | 80%; the remaining 20% to a test-suite rewrite their platform team owns |
| Dependency | A change freeze covering their peak trading window in November |

The GitLab public handbook publishes objective examples in exactly this shape — baseline, measurable
success criteria, business impact — and requires an objective to carry a clearly stated goal,
measurable success criteria, a timeline, and named owners and stakeholders before it is real
`[PROD]`.

## 10. Recording the objective

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `objective_statement` | The customer's words. No product nouns. If you cannot write it without naming a feature, you are one rung too low |
| `source` and `source_date` | Where it came from and when they said it. A quote with a date beats a paraphrase |
| `linked_customer_priority` | The public or internal priority it maps to |
| `executive_owner` · `measured_on` | Named person, and the metric their performance cycle contains |
| `customer_owner` · `vendor_owner` | Named humans on both sides |
| `state` | `Proposed` until the customer has agreed objective, baseline, success criteria and timeline |
| `confidence` | High only where the executive stated the objective themselves and the baseline is measured |

Cap the plan at **3–5 active objectives**; more than five means none of them are real `[P]`.

## 11. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Arriving with a finished plan to be approved | Arrive with questions; the conversation is the work |
| Objectives written from the sales handover without confirmation | Handover objectives start at `Proposed` and stay there until the customer restates them |
| Climbing the ladder to the mission | Stop one rung below; a mission has no baseline |
| Asking ten discovery questions | Three, then silence |
| Merging three stakeholders into one objective | Record all three, grade on the economic buyer's |
| Inventing the executive metric because it seemed obvious | Ask, or label the inference and its rule, and invite correction |
| Treating "no articulated objective" as a blocker | Make *establish the objective and baseline* the first deliverable |
| Objectives that name a feature | Rewrite from the outcome; features live in the product-behaviour row |
| Skipping the unit economic during discovery | Ask for it now; asking a year later reads as case-building |
| Recording a paraphrase instead of a quote | Quote them, with the date, and let them correct it |
