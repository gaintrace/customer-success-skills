# The Advocacy Ladder

> Ten rungs, lowest ask to highest. Each carries a cost and a risk **on both sides**, a readiness
> floor, an approval chain with a real lead time, and a set of gates. The ladder is not a menu:
> the top rungs cost the customer more than they cost you, so they are earned rather than
> requested — and a customer's first ask is never rung 8.
>
> Evidence labels: `[M]` measured · `[V]` vendor · `[P]` practitioner · `[A]` regulatory.

## The ladder at a glance

| # | Rung | Their time | Their risk | Approval chain | Calendar lead | Min readiness | Repeatable? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Survey / NPS | 2 min | None | None | Same day | 40 | Quarterly |
| 2 | Testimonial quote | 15 min | Name on a sentence | Them, sometimes comms | 1–2 weeks | 40 | Annually |
| 3 | Review-site review | 20–30 min | Public, permanent, searchable | Them; some employers restrict | 1–3 weeks | 55 | Annually |
| 4 | Logo use | 0 min | Brand adjacency | Legal or comms | 2–6 weeks | 55 | Once, term-bounded |
| 5 | Case study | 2–4 hrs | Their numbers in public | Them → comms → legal → their exec | **6–12 weeks** `[P]` | 65 | Every 18 months |
| 6 | Webinar / joint content | 3–5 hrs | Live, recorded, unscripted Q&A | Comms + their manager | 4–8 weeks | 70 | Twice a year |
| 7 | Conference speaking | 8–20 hrs + travel | Reputational, public, on video | Comms + exec + travel budget | 8–16 weeks | 75 | Once a year |
| 8 | Sales reference call | 30–45 min, recurring | Repeated cost their exec never sees | Them; occasionally legal | Days | 75 | **Max 4/yr, 1/quarter** |
| 9 | Advisory board | 6–10 hrs/yr, 2-yr term | NDA, exec exposure, opportunity cost | Their exec + legal | 8–12 weeks | 85 | Term-based |
| 10 | Co-development | Ongoing, contractual | Their roadmap bets on ours | Legal, security, their CTO | 3–6 months | 85 | Contractual |

**Read the "their risk" column before the "their time" column.** Rung 3 costs twenty minutes and
carries permanent public exposure; rung 8 costs half an hour and carries a cost their own
management never sees, which is precisely why it is the rung that burns people.

## 1 · Survey / NPS response

**Gates:** none beyond D1–D3. **Ask by:** in-product or email, automated. **Owner:** digital CS.

The only rung that scales without a human, and the one that feeds every other rung — a promoter
response from a live contact with standing opens a 7–14 day window for a rung 2–3 ask. Route it
within that window or the disposition decays.

**Do not** treat the survey as an advocacy act in the register. It is an input, not a spend
against the pool cap.

## 2 · Testimonial quote

**Gates:** D1–D3, D14. **Lead time:** 1–2 weeks. **Owner:** CSM drafts, customer edits.

Write the first draft from something they already said — a QBR line, a survey verbatim, an email —
and send it as *"here's what I heard you say; change anything, including to nothing"*. A quote you
compose from scratch reads like a quote you composed from scratch, and it is the fastest way to
lose the next ask.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Quote their number, their words, their date | Insert a superlative they never used |
| Give them the exact placement (web page, deck slide, one-pager) | Say "for marketing purposes" |
| Get title and company spelling confirmed in writing | Assume the title on the CRM record is current |
| Re-confirm annually, and when they change role | Leave a quote up after they leave the company |

## 3 · Review-site review

**Gates:** D1–D3, D5, D14. **Lead time:** 1–3 weeks. **Owner:** customer marketing.

This is the rung with statutory exposure, and the rules are not negotiable.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials**, effective 21 Oct 2024 `[A]` | Bans incentives contingent on a review being positive or expressing a particular sentiment — express **or implied** — and the reviewer's own disclosure does not cure it. Insider reviews (employees, their immediate family) require clear disclosure of the relationship. Civil penalties run up to **$51,744 per violation** |
| **G2 Community Guidelines** `[V]` | Incentive value capped at **$100 USD** across cash, gift cards, swag, credits and subscriptions; eligibility "never based on the opinions, positive or negative, expressed in the review"; confirmed incentivised reviews carry a permanent **"Incentivized"** tag; employees of the product's company and of direct competitors may not review; reseller reviews are labelled **"Business Partner"** and excluded from the G2 Score |
| **Employer policy** | Some organisations forbid staff from publicly reviewing vendors. Ask before you ask |

**The safe construction:** offer the same thank-you to everyone who completes a review, state that
it is unconditional in the message itself, and never see the draft before it publishes. If your
company's programme cannot say that sentence honestly, fix the programme before the next ask.

**Freshness matters more than volume.** Reviews age visibly on every major platform, and a profile
whose newest review is fourteen months old reads worse to a buyer than one with half the count and
three from this quarter. Plan a small, steady cadence rather than an annual campaign.

## 4 · Logo use

**Gates:** D1–D3, D8. **Lead time:** 2–6 weeks (legal, not effort). **Owner:** legal + marketing.

Costs the customer no time and carries the highest ratio of legal exposure to effort on the
ladder. Three failures account for almost all of it:

| Failure | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| Using a logo after the contract ends | Term-bound the permission in writing; review the wall quarterly against active contracts |
| Using it in a context they did not approve (paid ads, a comparison page, an investor deck) | Enumerate the placements in the permission, and treat anything not enumerated as not permitted |
| Relying on a champion's verbal yes | The champion rarely owns brand. Get it from legal or comms, in writing |

Where the MSA has a mutual-marketing clause, this is administrative. Where it does not, treat it
as a rung 4 ask with the same care as a case study.

## 5 · Case study / public story

**Gates:** D1–D9, D14, D15 — and the quantified outcome test. **Lead time: 6–12 weeks** through
customer review, legal sign-off and quote approval `[P]`. **Owner:** customer marketing.

**Start the clock when you ask, not when they say yes.** The most common failure is a campaign
that assumed two weeks, which turns a willing customer into a chased one.

| Stage | Owner | Typical | Accelerator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask, scope and outcome agreed | CSM | 1 week | The outcome already exists from a QBR |
| Interview | Customer marketing | 45–60 min | Send questions 48 hours ahead |
| Draft and their review | Marketing → champion | 1–3 weeks | Draft from the interview transcript, not from a template |
| Their comms review | Their comms | 1–4 weeks | Ask for the vendor-mention policy at the ask |
| Their legal review | Their legal | 1–4 weeks | Offer the anonymised version **before** they ask for it |
| Their exec sign-off | Their exec | 1–2 weeks | Their exec already saw the number in the QBR |

**Offer the anonymised version first, unprompted.** "A $2bn logistics operator" published in six
weeks beats a named story that dies in legal after fourteen. In regulated verticals, assume the
anonymised version is the outcome and treat a named one as upside.

## 6 · Webinar / joint content

**Gates:** rung 5 gates plus a live-risk check. **Lead time:** 4–8 weeks. **Owner:** marketing.

The distinguishing risk is unscripted: they will be asked something you have not prepared them
for, in public, by a stranger. **Prepare the customer, not the slides** — send the three questions
you expect from the audience, the two you hope nobody asks, and your honest answer to both, so
they are never the one improvising a defence of your product.

Never let a webinar carry a product announcement the customer has not seen. Being used as the
credibility wrapper for a launch they learned about on the call ends the relationship's advocacy.

---

## 7 · Conference speaking

**Gates:** rung 6 gates plus travel, budget and their exec. **Lead time:** 8–16 weeks.

The highest-effort rung short of governance, and the one where **our** risk is largest: the slot
moves, the track is cut, the session is scheduled opposite the keynote — after they booked flights
and told their team. Confirm the slot in writing before the customer commits anything, and if it
moves, tell them the same day and offer to cover the change fee (`R19`, `R20`).

The return is disproportionately **theirs**: a speaking credential, a recorded talk, a profile
their own promotion case can cite. Frame the ask that way and it is often accepted by people who
decline everything else.

## 8 · Sales reference call

**Gates:** D1–D6, D10–D13. **Lead time:** days. **Owner:** CSM or the reference desk.

Repeatable, in constant demand, and therefore **the rung that burns people**. Its risk is
invisible: half an hour a quarter looks trivial to whoever is asking, and the customer's own
manager never sees the cumulative cost.

| Control | Default `[P]` |
| --- | --- |
| Calls per customer per year | 4 |
| Calls per customer per quarter | 1 |
| Minimum gap between calls | 45 days |
| Advance notice before a call | 5 working days |
| Briefing before every call | Who the prospect is, why they asked, what they will ask, and what we have already told them |

**Give them the right of refusal per prospect, not once at the start.** "You'll see who they are
before you agree" costs nothing and is the single line that keeps advocates in the pool.

**Never brief a customer on what to say.** Prepare them on what they will be *asked*. The
difference is the whole credibility of the rung, and prospects detect the coached version
immediately. `[M]` TrustRadius / Pavilion's *2024 B2B Buying Disconnect* (2,164 buyers, 243
vendors, Mar–Apr 2024) found **56% of buyers had conversations with product users before
purchasing — 71% of enterprise buyers** — while only **27%** used vendor-supplied references and
**50%** went to former colleagues or known peers. Buyers reach for the unmanaged conversation
first; a coached reference confirms why.

## 9 · Customer Advisory Board

**Gates:** readiness ≥85, D1–D9, exec sponsor engaged. **Lead time:** 8–12 weeks. **Owner:** CCO.

**The mistake this rung exists to prevent: running the board as a roadmap focus group.** A CAB is
a strategy forum where senior customers meet each other and shape direction; a roadmap review is a
product ritual with a different audience and a different agenda. Conflating them produces a room
of executives being asked to prioritise features, which is the fastest way to lose them.

| Element | Practitioner convention `[P]` |
| --- | --- |
| Size | 12–20 member companies |
| Attendee | One executive per company — not the admin, not a delegate |
| Charter, written | Purpose · term (typically 2 years, staggered) · confidentiality/NDA · **what the board does not decide** |
| Cadence | Annual, bi-annual or quarterly, stated in the charter |
| Agenda split | ≤40% vendor presenting, ≥60% customer discussion |
| Between sessions | A published **"you said / we did"** artifact before the next meeting — the strongest single predictor of members returning |
| Selling | Never in the room, and no commercial ask inside 30 days either side |
| Composition | Never invite an at-risk account to repair the relationship. The board is earned, not therapeutic |

**Charter clause worth writing explicitly:** *"the board advises on direction and priorities; it
does not approve, schedule or commit product roadmap."* It protects you from `R19` and protects
them from the awkwardness of having their advice visibly ignored.

**A resignation from the board is a P0 signal.** Run `churn-risk` the same week.

## 10 · Co-development / design partner

**Gates:** readiness ≥85, exec sponsor, security review, and a contract. **Lead time:** 3–6 months.

The only rung where the customer takes production risk on your roadmap. It needs a written
agreement covering scope, IP, exclusivity window, support commitments, and — most importantly —
**what happens if the feature is not shipped**. Without that clause you have converted your
strongest advocate into your most exposed customer.

Never enter a design partnership to save an account. A customer in trouble needs the product they
already bought to work.

## Legal and approvals

| Approval | Whose | Typical lead | Ask for it at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publicity / logo permission | Their legal | 2–6 weeks | The ask, not the launch |
| Vendor-mention policy | Their comms | Days, if you ask | The ask |
| Quote approval | The speaker + their manager | 1–2 weeks | Draft stage |
| Named-company approval | Their exec | 1–2 weeks | Draft stage |
| Anonymised fallback | Their legal | Usually immediate | Offer unprompted |
| Security review (rung 10) | Their security | 2–6 weeks | Before any commitment |
| IR timing (public companies) | Their IR | Days | Before scheduling anything dated |

**Our side has approvals too.** Nothing referencing a customer's numbers publishes without the CSM
confirming the figure is theirs and current, and nothing with a date in it publishes without the
named owner of that date agreeing (`R19`).

## Per-model rungs

| Model | Rungs that work | Rungs that do not | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Enterprise / annual** | All ten | — | The full ladder, with three vetoes to find |
| **Product-led / self-serve** | 1–3, plus in-product prompts | 8–10 without recruitment | No champion by default; a named reference requires pulling a human out of the base first |
| **Consumption** | 1–8 | 5 framed on volume | A volume story reads as a bill; lead on workload breadth and time-to-first-production-workload |
| **Regulated vertical** | 1–3, 8, 9 | 4–7 without a policy check | Vendor-mention policies; expect the anonymised outcome |
| **Partner / channel-led** | 1–3, 8 with the partner present | 4–7 unilaterally | The end customer is not yours to publish, and review platforms label partner reviews separately |
| **Public company (theirs)** | All, outside quiet periods | Anything dated inside a blackout | IR timing governs, absolutely |

## What each rung is worth

Two numbers, reported separately and never summed.

| Rung | Sourced value | Influenced value | Honest caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | None directly | Small; feeds every other rung | Survey supply is the pool's input, not an output |
| 3 | Inbound attributable to the profile | Meaningful and unmeasurable at deal level | Buyers read reviews before they identify themselves |
| 4 | None | Brand-level | Logo walls are table stakes, not a differentiator |
| 5–7 | Content-attributed pipeline | Large, late-stage | A story read in week two gets no credit in the CRM |
| 8 | None — it never sources | **Highest per unit**, exclusively late-stage | The attribution trap: a reference call late in a cycle takes credit for a deal it did not create |
| 9–10 | Referrals originating in the room | Retention, not pipeline | Members renew at higher rates *because they were selected for health* |

**The measurement you can defend:** customer-sourced pipeline — opportunities whose source field
records a referral or introduction from an existing customer. Everything else is influence, which
is real, unmeasurable at deal level without a holdout, and must be labelled as such.
`[P]` The same confounding applies to any "advocates retain better" claim: you chose your
healthiest customers. Match on segment, ARR band, tenure and prior-period health, or say
correlational and stop.
