# Advocacy Ask Templates

> Worked, send-ready copy blocks across the ladder, plus the reply when they say no and the note that
> repairs a burned advocate. Written to `../../cs-context/references/customer-voice.md`: warmth is
> specificity, the firewall is absolute, and every block is plain text for an email client with **no
> unfilled placeholders**. The names, numbers and dates below are worked examples — replace them. A
> block that still says "your team" or "[Name]" is not send-ready: drop that sentence and raise the
> gap above the divider as `UNKNOWN — requires X`.

## The shape of a good ask

Six elements. A message missing any one of them converts worse, and missing three reads as a form.

| # | Element | The test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Their achievement, in their number and their date** | Could this first line have gone to any of forty customers? Then rewrite it |
| 2 | **The ask, precisely** | What, how long, to whom, and where it appears. "Would you be open to helping with marketing?" is not an ask |
| 3 | **Approval rights, stated first** | They approve every word before it goes anywhere. Saying this up front removes the largest objection before they raise it |
| 4 | **What they get, concretely** | "Exposure" is not a benefit. Two named introductions is |
| 5 | **A pre-built graceful decline** | *"If the timing is wrong, say 'not this quarter'."* It costs one line and it is why they answer at all |
| 6 | **One ask** | A message with two asks gets the easier one answered and the harder one forgotten |

**Send it from the person who has the relationship**, not a shared marketing address. Rung 9 is the
exception: an advisory board invitation comes from the CCO, because the sender's seniority is part of
the offer.

## What never crosses

Run the leak scan in `../../cs-context/references/customer-voice.md` over every block. None of this
reaches the customer, in any wording, however softened (`R18`): readiness score or band · risk band or
health score · ARR, exposure or pool tier · "referenceable" · fatigue index or "you've been asked three
times this year" · the pool, the rotation, the caps, the register · why they were selected over another
customer · anything about the prospect's deal size, stage or close date · any assessment of a named
person on their side or ours.

**The cap is a promise, not a disclosure.** "Twice, and you can stop after the first" is warm and
send-ready. "You're at 3 of your 4 annual reference calls" is the register leaking.

## Block 1 — rung 2, testimonial confirmation

**When:** within 14 days of a customer milestone, or after a promoter response.
**The move:** draft it from something they already said, then hand them the pen.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: The line you said in the June review — can I use it?

Hi Marcus,

In the June review you said the September run went from "three people
and a weekend" to one person and ninety minutes. That's stuck with me.

I'd like to use it, in your words, on one page of our site and in one
sales one-pager. Here's exactly what I'd publish:

  "It used to take three people and a weekend. It's now one person
   and ninety minutes."
  Marcus Vella, Head of Operations, Kestrel Logistics

Change any of it, including to nothing. Nothing goes anywhere until
you reply "yes to this version".

If you'd rather not, that's a clean no and I won't ask again this year.

Thanks,
Sam
```

## Block 2 — rung 3, review ask

**When:** 7–14 days after a promoter response or a verified milestone.
**The constraint:** the incentive may not depend on what they write. Say so in the message.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Twenty minutes, and I'll take the write-up off your plate

Hi Priya,

Your team hit 94% first-pass accuracy on the September run, up from
71% in March when you started. That's your number, from your QC
report, not ours.

I'd like to ask you to write that up as a review on G2. Twenty
minutes, your words, published under your name and Halden's. You'd
write it directly on the platform — I never see it first, and
whatever you say stands, including the parts we got wrong.

Two things worth covering, if it helps you start:

  • What the September run looked like before and after
  • The one thing you'd tell someone evaluating us to check first

G2 sends a thank-you gift card for any completed review through their
programme. It arrives whatever you write, and reviews from that
programme carry an "incentivized" label — I'd rather tell you that
up front than have you find it afterwards.

If you'd rather not, no follow-up from me.

Best,
Sam
```

**Why this one is safe:** the thank-you is unconditional and said to be unconditional, the label is
disclosed before they write rather than after, and the author never sees the draft. `[A]` The FTC Rule
on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (effective 21 Oct 2024) prohibits incentives contingent
on a review's sentiment, express or implied; `[V]` G2's Community Guidelines cap incentive value at
$100 and tag confirmed incentivised reviews permanently.

## Block 3 — rung 5, case study ask

**When:** a customer-stated quantified outcome exists and no disqualifier is live.
**The move:** name the real lead time and offer the anonymised version before their legal team does.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Writing up the dock-to-stock result — 45 minutes of yours

Hi Dana,

Dock-to-stock went from 31 hours to 9 across the three sites, and you
put that number in front of your own board in July. I'd like to
write it up properly.

What it costs you: one 45-minute call with me and our writer, and one
read of the draft. We write everything; you delete anything.

How it works, in order:

  • We talk for 45 minutes, questions sent 48 hours ahead
  • We draft, you edit, nothing is final until you say so
  • Your comms and legal teams see it before anyone else does
  • You approve the final version, including the headline number

Honestly on timing: with two approval chains on your side this
usually takes eight to twelve weeks end to end, so I'd rather start
it now than promise you something quick.

If your comms team has a vendor-mention policy, send it over and
I'll write inside it. And if a named story is difficult, we can
publish it as "a national three-site logistics operator" — that
version works for us and needs far less of your legal team.

Would the week of 15 September work for the 45 minutes?

Thanks,
Jo
```

## Block 4 — rung 8, reference call ask

**When:** readiness ≥75, inside the rest interval, fit cell matched, renewal resolved.
**The move:** bound the commitment, give the per-prospect veto, and lead with what they get.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Asking you for something specific — 30 minutes, twice

Hi Dana,

Your team closed Q3 in four days against eleven in April. That's the
number you said you'd judge this on, and it's yours, not ours.

The ask: two 30-minute calls with operations leaders at companies
about your size, who are roughly where you were in April. I'd brief
you on each one first, you'd see who they are before you agree, and
you can stop after the first.

You won't be briefed on what to say. Only on what they're likely to
ask, so nothing lands cold.

What you get back either way: an introduction to Priya Nair at
Kestrel, who solved the intercompany close step you're still doing by
hand. That happens whether or not you take the calls — I'll make it
this week.

If the timing is wrong, say "not this quarter" and I'll ask again in
January.

Thanks — I know close week is brutal.

Jo
```

## Block 5 — rung 9, advisory board invitation

**When:** readiness ≥85, exec sponsor engaged, and the customer is not in a save.
**Sent by:** the CCO, not the CSM. **The move:** state the term, the time, and what the board does
*not* decide — the last of those is what makes senior people say yes.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Customer Advisory Board — inviting you for the 2027-28 term

Hi Dana,

I'd like to invite you onto our Customer Advisory Board for the
two-year term starting January 2027.

Sixteen companies, one operations or technology executive from each.
You'd be the fourth logistics operator in the room and the only one
running a three-site intercompany close, which is exactly the gap in
the current group.

What it takes: four 90-minute sessions a year, one of them in person,
plus about 30 minutes of reading beforehand. Roughly eight hours over
twelve months.

What the board does: tells us where the market is going, pressure-
tests our direction, and decides what we should stop doing. What it
does not do: approve, schedule or commit our roadmap. I'd rather be
straight about that than have you spend a year advising into a
process you can't see.

What members tell us they get from it: the other fifteen people. Two
of the current members ran a joint pilot together that had nothing to
do with us.

The charter, the confidentiality terms and the session dates are
attached. Membership is personal to you, not to Meridian, so it moves
with you if you do.

Can I get 20 minutes in the next two weeks to talk it through?

Best,
Amara Diallo
Chief Customer Officer
```

## Block 6 — the pre-call briefing

**Sent before every reference call, without exception.** Preparing them on what will be *asked* is fine; preparing them on what to *say* is coaching, and prospects detect it instantly.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Thursday 2pm — who you're talking to

Hi Dana,

Thursday's call is with Tomas Berg, Head of Supply Chain Operations
at Verdanta Foods. Four sites, similar intercompany problem, about
where you were eighteen months ago.

He asked for this call himself. He'll likely ask:

  • How long implementation actually took, start to useful
  • What broke, and how we handled it
  • Whether you'd buy it again at the price you paid

Say whatever's true, including the parts that were painful. The July
outage is fair game — he'll trust the rest more for hearing it.

You don't need to prepare anything. If you'd rather not take it after
all, tell me by Wednesday and I'll move it with no explanation owed.

Thanks,
Jo
```

## Block 7 — the reply when they say no

**The most important block in this file.** How you take a no determines whether there is ever a yes. Reply the same day, remove the ask entirely, and give something.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: Re: Asking you for something specific

Understood, Dana — that's a clean no and it costs you nothing with me.

The introduction to Priya Nair still stands. I'll make it Thursday
unless you say otherwise.

I'll leave this alone until January. If something changes before
then, you know where I am.

Jo
```

**Then do the internal work.** A decline is data (Step 8 of `../SKILL.md`):

| What happened | Read it as | Route to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First decline, reason given, timing-related | Timing. Re-eligible in 90 days | Register, next-eligible date |
| First decline, no reason | Champion standing may have changed | One question, no ask attached |
| **Agreed, then withdrew** | **Advocacy withdrawal — P0** | `churn-risk`, this week |
| Second decline in 12 months | Out of the pool for two quarters (`R21`) | Register, plus a repair conversation with no ask in it |
| Redirected to someone junior | The relationship dropped a level | `stakeholder-map` |

## Block 8 — the repair note

**When:** an advocate was over-asked, mismatched, unrewarded or exposed. Diagnose which before writing
— the four burns need different sentences; full diagnosis in `pool-management.md`. **Rules for this
note:** name it in the first line, apologise once (`R20`), attach no ask, hand back control, and state
the freeze with a date.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CUSTOMER-FACING — copy the block below and send as written.
Everything above this line is internal. Do not forward it.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

```text
Subject: I've asked you for too much

Hi Dana,

Four reference calls since February, plus the webinar. That's more
than I should have asked of anyone, and you said yes to all of it
because you're generous, not because it was reasonable.

I'm stopping. Nothing from me on this until at least March, and when
I do come back it'll be one thing a year with the dates agreed with
you in advance rather than whenever we need it.

Two things I owe you and haven't delivered: the intercompany
walkthrough I promised in June, and the introduction to Priya Nair.
Both this month.

No reply needed to this one.

Jo
```

## Phrases that kill an ask

| Never write | Write instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Just checking in to see if you'd be open to..." | The specific observation, then the specific ask | No content; a request for attention with nothing offered |
| "We'd love to feature you as a customer success story!" | "Dock-to-stock went from 31 hours to 9. I'd like to write it up." | Our excitement, their obligation |
| "It would be great exposure for your brand" | "A speaking credential, a recorded talk, and a byline you own" | Exposure is what people offer when they have nothing to offer |
| "This will only take a few minutes" | "45 minutes, plus one read of the draft" | Understating the cost is the fastest way to lose the second ask |
| "As a valued partner, we'd like to ask..." | Their achievement, named | An assertion of the relationship where evidence belongs |
| "Leave us a 5-star review and we'll send you a gift card" | "The thank-you arrives whatever you write" | Prohibited outright: incentives may not be contingent on sentiment `[A]` |
| "We'd need this by Friday for a campaign" | "Eight to twelve weeks with two approval chains — I'd rather start now" | Our deadline is not their problem, and it names us as the priority |
| "Can you hop on a quick call with a prospect?" | "Two 30-minute calls; you'll see who they are before you agree" | Unbounded, un-vetoed, and it hides the recurrence |
| "You're one of our best references" | Silence — this is register language | Tells them they are being managed as a resource |
| "Since we've done so much for you this year..." | Nothing. Delete the sentence | Reciprocity invoked out loud reads as debt collection |
| "Our CEO would really appreciate it" | The value to them | Moves the benefit to our side of the table |
| "Let me know your thoughts" | "Would the week of 15 September work?" | A closed question is easier to answer than an invitation |
