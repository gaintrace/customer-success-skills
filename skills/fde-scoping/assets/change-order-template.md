# Change Order — template

> **Customer-facing.** One page. Raised the **same day** as the request — a change order raised
> three weeks later, for work already done, is a bill for a favour and it is remembered.
>
> Nothing internal crosses into it: no three-point workings, no contingency, no margin, no
> absorbed-hours ledger, no assessment of anyone (`R18`). It carries the change, the criterion,
> the effort, the fee effect and the dates.
>
> **Fill every `<slot>` or delete the sentence that needed it.** Never leave a placeholder.

## Covering note — send with it, same day

Keep it to five lines. The change order is the record; this is the conversation.

```text
Hi <name>,

Here is the write-up for the <thing> you asked about on <day> — worth doing, and I
would rather have it in.

It is <n> hours and it moves milestone <n> from <date> to <date>. If you would
rather keep the date, we can swap it for <the scoped item nobody has asked about>
instead, or park it for phase 2 — happy either way, just tell me which.

Nothing else changes.

<Your name>
```

## Schedule change order — the no-fee variant

Issue this whenever a dependency on the customer's side slips by more than three working days.
Dates only, no fee change, no blame. It is what makes the conversation at the end of the
engagement possible.

```text
Hi <name>,

Short note for the record so the dates stay clear.

Milestone <n> moves from <date> to <date>. The <service account request / field
mapping sign-off> raised on <date> (<ticket ref>) is still open with your <team>,
and <the work it blocks> cannot start until it lands. Everything after it shifts by
the same amount.

No change to the fee, and nothing else in the plan changes.

If it would help, I am happy to join the call with your <team> — it sometimes moves
faster when we can answer the technical questions live.

<Your name>
```
